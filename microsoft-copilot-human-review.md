# Does a person review Microsoft Copilot prompts?

As of 2 November 2026, this note does not say a person never sees a prompt. [https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy) says abuse monitoring, which includes human review of content, is available in Azure OpenAI, and Microsoft Copilot services have opted out of it. That sentence does not say prompts, responses, employees, contractors, a default, or a customer control.
Updated 2 November 2026: when read on 14 October 2026 the Copilot privacy page also carried a note that Microsoft 365 Copilot is now named Microsoft Copilot, with no changes to security, compliance, and privacy for organizations; that note is gone as of 2 November 2026, and the page's metadata gives an update date of 31 October 2026, though its visible "Last updated on" line still reads 18 August 2026. The page was re-read on 2 November 2026 and the abuse-monitoring sentence is unchanged.

Last checked: 2 November 2026. Every page below was re-read that day.

Updated 2 November 2026 (second change): the repeated question line under the heading was removed. No claims changed.

[https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy) (canonical [https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy), page dated 9 July 2026; visible date 4 November 2026 Adelaide (was 18 August 2026) and metadata updated 5 November 2026 Adelaide; re-read 5 November 2026, and the abuse-monitoring sentence is unchanged) says abuse monitoring, which includes human review of content, is available in Azure OpenAI, and Microsoft Copilot services have opted out of it. That sentence does not say prompts, responses, employees, contractors, a default, or a customer control. The page now uses the name Microsoft Copilot throughout. When read on 14 October 2026 the page also carried a note that Microsoft 365 Copilot is now named Microsoft Copilot, with no changes to security, compliance, and privacy for organizations; that note is gone as of 2 November 2026. Separate sentences describe content filters and classifiers that analyze inputs and block high-risk prompts. Those sentences do not say a person reviews them. A team reviews AI systems, not customer prompts for abuse monitoring.

[https://www.microsoft.com/licensing/faqs/123](https://www.microsoft.com/licensing/faqs/123) says abuse monitoring terms do not apply to Microsoft 365 Copilot or Microsoft Copilot Chat. It does not say whether a person reviews prompts or responses. A Fabric answer on that page is not about Microsoft 365 Copilot, so this note does not quote it as one.

This note does not say a person never sees a prompt, and it does not recommend a plan.
