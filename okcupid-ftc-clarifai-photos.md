# OkCupid gave Clarifai nearly three million photos, FTC alleges

The FTC alleges that in 2014 OkCupid gave Clarifai, Inc. access to nearly three million user photos, plus demographic and location data, without the opt-out its privacy policy promised. The defendants, Match Group Americas, LLC and Humor Rainbow, Inc. (d/b/a OkCupid), neither admit nor deny the allegations. The settlement order bans misrepresenting privacy practices for 20 years. It sets no money. The FTC documents do not say the photos were used to train AI; that account comes from news reports, quoted separately below.

Last checked: 2 November 2026. The FTC case page, press release, complaint and stipulated order were read that day via search.ftc.gov. The CourtListener docket and the Reuters report (via its Yahoo Finance copy) were read that day.

Filed version cited; entered order sits behind PACER. PACER is the US federal courts' paid records system. The order the judge entered on 30 April 2026 (docket entry 17) is there; this note quotes the stipulated order as filed on 30 March 2026.

## What the FTC alleges

These are allegations in the complaint, not findings by a court.

The policy promise. "Humor Rainbow set forth that it “do[es] not share your personal information with others except as indicated in this Privacy Policy or when we inform you and give you an opportunity to opt out of having your personal information shared.”" (Complaint ¶40, p. 7)

The request. "In September 2014, the CEO of Clarifai, Inc. e-mailed one of OkCupid’s founders requesting that Humor Rainbow give Clarifai, Inc. (i.e., the Data Recipient) access to large datasets of OkCupid photos." (Complaint ¶32, p. 6)

No opt-out, no notice. "Humor Rainbow did not give users an opportunity to opt out of having their personal information shared, as it stated it would in its privacy policy. Indeed, Match and Humor Rainbow never informed users that Humor Rainbow shared their personal information." (Complaint ¶43, p. 7)

No restrictions. "Humor Rainbow never executed a formal agreement or set forth restrictions governing the Data Recipient’s access to, or use of, the OkCupid user data." (Complaint ¶36, p. 6)

## What the order requires

Section I (p. 3) says the defendants "are permanently restrained and enjoined from misrepresenting or assisting others in misrepresenting, expressly or by implication: A. The extent to which Defendants collect, maintain, use, disclose, or delete, or protect any Covered Information; B. The purpose(s) for which Defendants collect, maintain, use, or disclose any Covered Information;" and (C) the function of privacy controls and consumer choices.

Section VI (p. 7): "this Order will remain in effect for 20 years after its entry by the Court". The remaining sections cover acknowledgments, compliance reports, recordkeeping and compliance monitoring.

The order does not require any payment and does not require Clarifai or the defendants to delete data. The quotes above are from the stipulated order as filed with the joint motion on 30 March 2026 (Document 3-1). This note did not read the text of the order as entered on 30 April 2026 (docket entry 17).

## What the news reports add, not the FTC

The FTC documents say "facial recognition technology company" and do not mention training. The training account comes from these reports:

- Reuters, Jody Godoy, 20 April 2026, read via the syndicated copy on [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-company-deleted-okcupid-user-210210873.html) ([archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20261102000304/https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-company-deleted-okcupid-user-210210873.html)): "Artificial intelligence company Clarifai said this month it had deleted 3 million OkCupid user photos and facial-recognition models trained on them". Reuters also reports "Clarifai certified to the FTC on April 7 that it had deleted the data, according to a document seen by Reuters." That is Clarifai's account as reported by Reuters. Reuters' own framing is that OkCupid settled "for providing photos and demographic data to train Clarifai's facial-recognition models in 2014". That wording is Reuters', not the FTC's.
- The New York Times, 2019, as quoted by Ars Technica on 31 March 2026: "Clarifai used the images from OkCupid to build a service that could identify the age, sex and race of detected faces, Mr. Zeiler said". ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/03/okcupid-match-pay-no-fine-for-sharing-user-photos-with-facial-recognition-firm/) ([archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20261102000420/https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/03/okcupid-match-pay-no-fine-for-sharing-user-photos-with-facial-recognition-firm/)))

## Open questions

- The FTC case page says "Pending"; the court docket shows the order entered and the case terminated on 30 April 2026. This note has not seen an FTC update.
- This note did not read the entered order (docket entry 17, PACER only) and has not compared it with the filed version.
- No FTC document we read says what Clarifai did with the photos, or confirms Clarifai's deletion.
- Clarifai is not a defendant. The order binds Match Group Americas, LLC and Humor Rainbow, Inc. and their officers, agents, employees and others in active concert with them. Section I (p. 3) covers "Defendants, Defendants' officers, agents, employees, and attorneys, and all other persons in active concert or participation with any of them, who receive actual notice of this Order".
